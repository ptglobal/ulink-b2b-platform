# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

ULink serves procurement managers, factory operators, quality teams, and business owners sourcing cleanroom and packaging materials across Vietnam. They often work under delivery, compliance, and production-continuity pressure and need to move from an uncertain requirement to a credible SKU, sample, quotation, or delivery plan quickly.

The same platform also serves ULink sales, content, and operations teams. Authenticated and admin routes override the default brand register with the product register: those users need compact, predictable workflows for RFQs, samples, orders, invoices, deliveries, catalog data, and publishing.

## Product Purpose

ULink turns industrial sourcing into a clear, traceable process. The public experience establishes capability and trust, helps buyers identify the correct solution or SKU, and moves qualified demand into sample or RFQ workflows. The portal and admin experience then keeps commercial activity, documents, fulfillment, and customer communication visible and actionable.

Success means a buyer can understand what ULink supplies, verify why it is credible, and take the next procurement action without ambiguity; internal teams can process that demand without relearning the interface on every screen.

## Brand Personality

Technical, assured, institutional.

ULink should feel like a group-level wholesale operating system: calm under pressure, precise with specifications, and visibly connected to factories, warehouses, regional hubs, procurement policy, and delivery schedules. The emotional goal is informed confidence. Carbon Design System is the shared visual and interaction language across public, portal, and administration surfaces.

## Anti-references

- Generic corporate brochure sites built around a full-screen stock photo and vague promises.
- SaaS-template hero sections with nested glass cards, oversized metrics, purple-blue gradients, or decorative dashboards.
- Sky-gradient admin panels, excessive pills, oversized rounded cards, and inconsistent component styling.
- Repeated three-card feature grids, tiny uppercase eyebrows above every section, and icon-plus-copy filler.
- Visual polish that hides SKU details, compliance evidence, fulfillment status, or the next procurement action.
- Carbon-inspired styling that copies the appearance but bypasses Carbon components, tokens, icons, grid, or interaction states.
- Reusing the same photograph or illustration for unrelated modules, products, people, or pages.

## Design Principles

1. **Show operational proof.** Use real products, specifications, standards, hub coverage, response expectations, and process state instead of broad claims.
2. **One next move.** Every page should make the most useful next procurement action obvious while keeping secondary paths available without competing.
3. **Carbon is the contract.** Use the official React components, icons, type tokens, themes, and 2x Grid before introducing a ULink extension.
4. **Density follows intent.** Marketing uses Carbon's wide grid; forms use wide or narrow; portal and administration use condensed, data-dense compositions.
5. **CMS owns business content.** Product, capability, proof, campaign, contact, and editorial content comes from Directus; locale files only own interface language.
6. **One asset, one role.** Every meaningful photograph or illustration has one registered placement. Logos and functional icons are exempt because they represent stable identities or actions.
7. **Consistency earns trust.** Shared tokens, components, states, and content patterns remain coherent across Vietnamese, English, and Japanese experiences.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Meet WCAG 2.1 AA for contrast, keyboard operation, focus visibility, labels, errors, and meaningful image alternatives. Support reduced motion, never encode state with color alone, preserve readable 65-75 character prose lines, and keep critical actions usable on narrow mobile screens. Vietnamese, English, and Japanese text must tolerate length and glyph differences without clipping or losing hierarchy.
